How it works & limits

  • Terrain: default is Mapterhorn: national airborne-LiDAR terrain models where open (AT ~1 m, DE ~1 m, CH 0.5–2 m, IT 2–10 m, …), Copernicus GLO-30 elsewhere. Alternative: Terrain Tiles on AWS (Mapzen/Joerd: EU-DEM 30 m, AT 10 m, SRTM). Rays every 0.5° of azimuth out to the chosen range, finest tiles near the point, earth curvature with refraction coefficient 0.13.
  • Sun: NOAA solar position algorithm with refraction; the sun counts as visible when its centre is above the terrain horizon (2-min steps, 1-min for the selected day).
  • Not included: trees, buildings (both models are bare earth), clouds and fog. Results are clear-sky potential. A forest edge or a neighbouring house can remove low winter sun completely — check on site, or raise “height above ground”.
  • Point on a slope or ridge: the model elevation at the exact point can be off (especially with 30 m data). If you know the real elevation, set “Elevation override”. “Ignore terrain <” stops the point shading itself through pixel noise.
  • Cross-check: the button recomputes everything with the other elevation source and shows the difference. Large differences = treat the result with care. Further references: PVGIS horizon (EU JRC), PeakFinder panorama, ShadeMap / Shadowmap (incl. buildings).
